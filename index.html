THE_QUIET.TXT 01 / ORIGINS & THE LOOP [ □ ]
01

THE QUIET

No one is coming to tell you what to do.

§ 1.0

A vast, procedural galaxy, an analogue starship, and the next coordinate. You read the instruments, pick a point of light, and set out into the dark to see what awaits.

When Alessandro Ghignola released Noctis IV in 2000, he built something singular: a space simulator with billions of stars generated deterministically from pure mathematical coordinates. You awaken as a Felysian stardrifter aboard a living biological vessel, free to wander anywhere across the cosmos.

The exploration loop is contemplative, atmospheric, and quiet. Read the stellar spectral class and distance. Engage the sub-light Vimana drive across the void. Deploy the planetary capsule down into an alien atmosphere, walk across an untouched horizon, and leave an entry in the starmap for the next explorer.

Noctis IV OM revives this experience for modern hardware. It unites the expanded features of Noctis IV Plus (panoramas, Moviemaker, celestial catalogs) with the native C++20 architecture of Noctis IV LR—running seamlessly on modern desktop systems and directly in the browser.

The galaxy is computed, not stored. The same coordinate can produce the same world again.

THE SHIP

A biological vessel you inhabit rather than menu through. The cabin is the interface: a central console, two right-hand bulkheads, and a cupola that opens onto the hull.

FIRST_FLIGHT.TXT 02 / COLD START TO FELYSIA [ □ ]
02

FIRST FLIGHT

A first flight that actually works.

§ 2.0

Noctis does not put a marker on the next objective. The ship is the interface. This is the shortest reliable route from a cold launch to standing on Felysia.

  1. 01

    Walk the cabin

    WASD moves; the mouse looks. The central console is the flight computer. The right-hand bulkhead holds the GOESnet terminal and the planetary atlas. F1 brings up the in-cockpit reminder.

  2. 02

    Lock the home system

    At the GOESnet console, type HELP for the syntax if you need it, then ST BALASTRACKONASTREYA. That locks the Felysian home system as your remote target.

  3. 03

    Cross the dark

    At the central console, press 5 for flight control, then 7 to engage the Vimana drive. The distance counter is the only progress bar you get.

  4. 04

    Choose a world

    In the destination system, open the planetary list on the left terminal. Press 5, then 8 for local approach. Read gravity, pressure, and atmosphere before you commit.

  5. 05

    Land

    Press 5, then 9 for the surface capsule. In the atlas, move the landing reticle with the arrow keys and press Enter.

  6. 06

    Step outside

    WASD to walk, L for the suit torch, V to cycle the visor, Space to fire the jetpack. Walk back into the capsule to return to orbit.

THE_INSTRUMENT.TXT 03 / ENGINE & COMPATIBILITY [ □ ]
03

UNDER THE HULL

The old game is the specification. The new engine is the work.

§ 3.0

The surface is deliberately 1990s: a 320×200 indexed framebuffer, flat-shaded polygons, a palette. Under it is a C++20 engine with fixed-tick simulation, explicit platform seams, and compatibility fixtures that decide what “finished” means.

Noctis IV has captivated nerds for decades, but keeping a DOS-era assembly and C codebase alive on modern hardware is notoriously demanding. After spending months reverse-engineering Sierra’s SCI engine, I saw the monumental porting work started by the community and asked myself two simple questions: Can I do this? and Why wouldn't I?

The original DOS executable and the LR porting baseline serve as our exact behavioral specification. Every planetary formula, star seed, and flight calculation is pinned against automated test fixtures to guarantee total historical fidelity, compiled natively to modern C++20 and WebAssembly.

Subtle, handcrafted visual enhancements—Rayleigh and Mie atmospheric scattering, day/night terminators, limb-darkened stars, procedural coronal streamers, and a physically modeled suit torch—sit cleanly atop the simulation without altering its underlying mathematical seeds.

Noctis IV OM implementation at a glance
LanguageC++20, CMake, Raylib, GLM; dependencies pinned through CPM.
SimulationFixed 55 ms ticks. Presentation advances independently.
GalaxyCoordinate-seeded. DOS-compatible PRNG. Twelve stellar classes.
Renderer8-bit indexed framebuffer, software rasterizer, Scale2x and CRT shader.
Audiominiaudio, a 4-voice generative synth, and a 6-channel mixer.
Saves401-byte native v1; legacy profile migration; atomic replacement.
TargetsLinux x86-64 (Wayland/X11), Windows x86-64, WebAssembly/WebGL2.
Tests57 CTest suites on Linux Clang/GCC and ASan/UBSan.

Determinism, precisely. Stars, systems, and surface seeds are pinned against the DOS reference. A few rasterized surface pixels still differ between compilers because of floating-point rounding; those hashes are accepted explicitly per platform and enforced in CI. Read Known issues ↗.

SIMULATION

One tick at a time

Engine-owned state, fixed 55 ms ticks, semantic input replay, and a 401-byte native save. The simulation does not care how fast the screen is drawn.

PRESENTATION

The look is the spec

An 8-bit indexed framebuffer and software polygon renderer, scaled by crisp pixels, Scale2x, or a CRT shader. 4:3, 16:10, and 16:9 are deliberate choices.

PLATFORM

Thin seams, same core

Raylib and GLFW on the desktop; Emscripten and WebGL2 in the browser; miniaudio underneath. The procedural core is shared, not forked.

QUESTIONS.TXT 08 / FREQUENTLY ASKED [ □ ]
08

QUESTIONS

The short answers.

§ 8.0
Is Noctis IV OM the original game?

It is an independent community hybrid of Noctis IV Plus and Noctis IV LR, built to faithfully recreate the original game's behavior on modern systems. Alessandro Ghignola created Noctis IV; this project preserves his full authorship and original WTOF license terms.

Do I need DOSBox or an original copy of Noctis?

No. The release archives are completely self-contained and run natively as modern C++20 or WebAssembly binaries. The original DOS executable serves as a behavioral verification reference during development, not a runtime dependency.

What does “OM” stand for?

Open Movement: the community continuation pairing Plus's rich feature set with LR's native C++ architecture, bringing the galaxy forward into the modern era.

Which platforms are supported?

Windows x86-64, Linux x86-64 with X11 or Wayland, and WebAssembly in a browser with WebGL2. There is no native macOS build yet; the browser build is the current route on macOS.

Can I bring my old saves forward?

Native builds can migrate a portable DOS/Plus profile with ./nivlr --prepare-user-data --migrate-from "/path/to/old Noctis folder". The importer never overwrites a destination file. Browser saves live in that browser's IndexedDB and are separate from desktop profiles.

Is it really deterministic?

Stars, systems, and surface seeds are pinned against the DOS reference, and the accepted per-platform results are enforced in CI. A few rasterized surface pixels can differ across compilers because of floating-point rounding; those differences are explicit, not ignored. Read known issues ↗.

Why are the graphics so low-resolution?

The game renders at 320×200 internally, as the original did. It scales with crisp pixels, Scale2x edge-directed upscaling, or a CRT shader. The low resolution is the look, not a limitation of the port.

Is it open source?

Yes, under the original WTOF terms and the project's provenance conditions. Keep the credits and license texts with any redistribution. The provenance register ↗ records the evidence.
