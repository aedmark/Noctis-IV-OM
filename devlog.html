[TELEMETRY] System Development Log

INIT_DATE: September 27, 2026

SOURCE_REPOSITORY

Development on the modern Noctis port has reached an important turning point. Most of the recent work has been deep inside the machinery of the game: random-number generators, clocks, input adapters, palettes, and software polygon renderers.

The original DOS source remains our historical reference, while Noctis IV LR provides the modern C++ foundation.

[UPDATE] A Native Build We Can Trust

The Linux version can now be built consistently with two different C++ compilers. It has automated tests, startup diagnostics, and checks for memory errors and other unsafe behavior.

[UPDATE] Separating Time from Frame Rate

The simulation now advances in fixed steps, independently of how long the computer takes to present each frame. Drawing the screen and advancing the universe are treated as separate jobs.

[UPDATE] The Stardrifter Moves

The Stardrifter's interstellar and local guidance systems have been separated from the drawing loop and tested as part of the actual game code. For the first canonical journey, the ship leaves its inherited starting position and travels to BALASTRACKONASTREYA, the confirmed parent star of FELYSIA.

The interstellar leg takes exactly 396 simulation ticks. The local leg takes another 392. The trip also exercises the original power and lithium recharge behavior, ending with plenty of fuel in reserve.

[UPDATE] Down to the Ground and Back

The Stardrifter now has a repeatable round trip between orbit and the surface. A headless pilot requests a landing on FELYSIA, rides the capsule through gravity and touchdown, walks away across the generated terrain, returns to the capsule, and lets it seal and lift back aboard.

[UPDATE] The Stardrifter Answers Back

The cockpit has had a less glamorous but very useful refit. We mapped every inherited ship screen, every onboard menu, the three information panels in the flight display, and the four original preferences.

The modern keyboard bridge had another quiet limitation: it threw away everything except lowercase letters and digits. Printable text now arrives intact without being entered twice when Raylib reports it through two paths.

[UPDATE] GOESnet Comes Home

The Stardrifter no longer has to step out of the game and ask a folder of DOS programs what a command means. GOESnet now lives inside Noctis. Star and planet searches, travel targets, dependency listings, guide reading, note writing, catalog editing, star lists, exports, help, and screen clearing all pass through native components with one shared language.

[UPDATE] The Camera Is Rolling

Moviemaker is back. F3 now opens its control panel in space or on a planet, and a recording becomes a numbered “deck” of bitmap frames.

The native version treats old footage carefully. If a deck already exists, it refuses to record over it and asks for another number.

[UPDATE] Windows Calls In

The required Windows lane now builds cleanly and passes 34 automated tests. The deterministic stars and systems still agree, old saves still migrate, new saves survive restarts, GOESnet still edits its catalogs, Moviemaker still protects existing footage, and the scripted flights and landing state still arrive where expected.

[UPDATE] Hardening, Documentation, and 1.0 Readiness

Following the release of v0.0.1-preview and verification of the lithium persistence fix, Milestone 8 turned its focus toward comprehensive stabilization. We audited the software rasterizer to eliminate off-screen flare buffer overflows, corrected flare blending arithmetic, eliminated font glyph underflows, and added defensive bounds checks across the rendering pipeline.

Empirical profiling established strict performance budgets: the software rasterizer completes in under 2 milliseconds per frame, preserving over 96% headroom under the 55 ms DOS simulation tick. We expanded documentation with a comprehensive troubleshooting guide, contributor guidelines, and release runbooks. Release and rollback rehearsals passed cleanly for both Linux and Windows packages, preparing Noctis IV OM for 1.0 General Availability.

[UPDATE] 1.0.1 Patch: Smooth Landings, Starlight Floors, and Visor Controls

Following the 1.0 General Availability release, community exploration on low-gravity worlds like star Oakenshield’s first planet revealed an unexpected stall during descent. We investigated and established a solid vertical acceleration floor to guarantee smooth touchdown regardless of planetary mass, along with an instant Escape abort back to the cockpit.

We also illuminated planets around very dim stars that previously went pitch-black, protected the ship’s targeted body from power-loss resets while the pilot is outside, and restored the Page Up and Page Down controls for suit helmet visor operation and GOES guide scrolling.

[UPDATE] 1.1.0: Lighting the Torch — Nightside Illumination and Crisp HUD Telemetry

Exploring the unlit nightside of planets or tidally locked worlds no longer requires fumbling in total darkness. Version 1.1.0 introduces the planetary Suit Torch headlamp, toggled at any time with the L key. The headlamp casts an authentic spotlight beam directly in front of the explorer, blending ambient illumination, radial beam focus, quadratic distance attenuation, and smooth surface normal shading in 32-bit RGBA color space.

In addition, transient HUD status messages (such as TORCH ON/OFF and MOUSELOOK notifications) now render at crisp native display resolution directly over the retro framebuffer, eliminating text blur. Under the hood, surface session quitting via Escape now seamlessly synchronizes the ship’s orbital situation and guards celestial coordinates against cold-restart resets, ensuring your expeditions can be saved and resumed anywhere across the galaxy.

[UPDATE] 1.3.0: The Aural Update — Procedural Audio Engine, Vimana Warp Acoustics, and Observation Deck Pacing

Space in Noctis IV has always been vast and contemplative; now, it has a voice. Version 1.3.0 introduces a completely self-contained procedural ambient audio engine and Foley soundscape, synthesized in real-time at 44.1 kHz 32-bit floating point with zero external audio assets or binary sound files.

Aboard the Stardrifter, explorers are grounded by a meditative 55 Hz cabin hum, warm sub-bass warmth, gentle harmonics, soft CRT terminal purrs, and dual-filtered pink noise life-support ventilation breathing. Stepping through the cupola out onto the roof in open space (ontheroof) transitions to an expansive cosmic silence with diffuse solar wind sweeps.

During propulsion maneuvers, the Vimana drive dynamically tracks physical hyperlight velocity: starting deep and low (~28 Hz) at ignition, accelerating and oscillating into a rhythmic gravitic pulse as speed climbs to cruising warp (~96 Hz), gently decelerating into orbital parking, and spooling down over a seamless 1.8-second decay without abrupt cuts.

On planetary surfaces, the procedural engine dynamically models atmospheric pressure: airless moons and asteroids (atmosphere == 0) immerse the player in eerie dead silence punctuated only by the suit’s internal life-support hum, while worlds with atmosphere produce realistic howling winds, gust dynamics, rain droplet textures, and rolling thunder. Tactile mechanical Foley brings exploration to life: a dual-click for the suit torch (L), micro-stepper servo hums for the visor (PgUp/PgDn), cold-gas thruster bursts for the jetpack (Space), and subtle regolith footsteps, with quick mute control via F9 or Ctrl+M.

Finally, version 1.3.0 eliminates the legacy DOS ROOFSPEED frame-limiter bypass, guaranteeing canonical 18.2 FPS (55 ms per tick) simulation pacing on the observation deck and fixing the runaway multi-thousand FPS acceleration bug on modern CPUs, while restoring pure WASD backward movement on the S key.

[UPDATE] 1.4.0: The Celestial & Display Update — Planetary Rotation, Timewarp Slider, and Modern Display

Version 1.4.0 brings the cosmos into living motion and modernizes presentation without compromising retro software rendering authenticity. Planetary bodies now dynamically rotate on their spin axes in real time during spaceflight, synchronized with the simulation clock while keeping day/night terminators physically locked to host stars.

An interactive high-DPI Timewarp Multiplier slider HUD now allows explorers to adjust simulation rates smoothly from 1x to 5000x via mouse drag, step buttons, or [ / ] bracket keys, with universal timewarp toggle (T / Shift+S) available aboard the cabin, on planetary surfaces, and on the observation deck.

Modern display support adds authentic 4:3 CRT aspect ratio letterboxing with perfectly round celestial bodies (F8 to cycle with 16:10 square and 16:9 stretch), true/borderless fullscreen toggle (F11 / Alt+Enter), dynamic window resizing, and high-DPI frosted glass HUD telemetry badges.

[UPDATE] 1.5.0: The Visual Fidelity & Post-Processing Update — Deterministic Upscaling, CRT Shader, Sub-Pixel Geometry, and Settings Persistence

Version 1.5.0 elevates visual fidelity on modern high-resolution displays while strictly preserving retro determinism and 100% regression hash parity. We introduced deterministic Scale2x / EPX edge-directed upscaling (F7) to expand the 320x200 canvas to 640x400 with diagonal smoothing and complete palette purity.

For authentic retro presentation, an OpenGL 3.3 GLSL CRT simulation shader (F6) faithfully reproduces subtle 200-line scanlines, aperture grille phosphor triads, gentle glass curvature, corner vignette shading, and celestial core glow within a tight 1.0 ms GPU budget.

In the 3D software rasterizer, we introduced sub-pixel geometry rasterization across polymap and poly3d, employing floating-point vertex projection, sub-pixel edge pre-stepping, and scanline endpoint rounding. This completely eliminates vertex crawling and jitter on planetary terrain, mountain meshes, and ship interior geometry, while bilinear point distribution in far_pixel_at eliminates discrete jumping when panning past distant stars.

Finally, user display preferences now automatically persist across sessions via display_settings.ini in platform configuration directories, seamlessly remembering aspect ratios, upscaling filters, CRT emulation, sub-pixel fidelity, fullscreen state, and timewarp multipliers.

[UPDATE] 1.6.0: The Image Archive Update — In-Cockpit Snapshot & Panorama Viewer

Version 1.6.0 brings the explorer's photo album aboard the Stardrifter. Press F4 in the cockpit to open the GOES Image Archive on the newest capture, then browse every snapshot and panorama with Left/Right, Page Up/Page Down, and Home/End. The GOES console learned two new commands: GALLERY lists the archive newest-first on the output screen, and VIEW n opens a specific image.

Each image is decoded with the palette saved inside its own BMP and shown at the same pixel aspect as the live view, drawn at full window resolution above the retro canvas so the game's palette is never disturbed. Wide surface panoramas can be zoomed to full height with Z and panned across with a position indicator.

Taking a snapshot now confirms itself with a brief high-resolution notice that never enters the indexed framebuffer, so it cannot appear in the next capture or in Moviemaker frames. We also fixed a long-standing quirk inherited from the DOS header template: ordinary snapshots declared stale file sizes that strict image tools rejected, and they now open everywhere.

[UPDATE] 1.7.0: The Web Update — Noctis IV OM in Your Browser

Version 1.7.0 takes the Stardrifter into the browser. The complete game now builds for WebAssembly with Emscripten and runs on the project website: the same deterministic 320x200 software renderer, cockpit, GOESnet console, planetary surfaces, image archive, and procedural audio, with nothing to install.

Rather than restructuring the inherited game loops, the browser build uses ASYNCIFY so the space and surface loops hand control back to the browser once per 55 ms tick. Rendering goes through WebGL2, so the CRT shader works in the browser too, and the canvas follows any window size while the game letterboxes its 4:3 frame.

Saves live in the browser's IndexedDB storage. The game autosaves about every 30 seconds and whenever the tab is hidden, including your exact position on a planet's surface, so closing the tab is safe. Because browsers reserve Esc for releasing the mouse and leaving fullscreen, it never ends a web session; on a planet it saves in place.

Along the way we fixed quick key taps shorter than one frame being ignored on every platform, and the site's manual links now open the preserved Noctis IV Plus manual. The site itself now deploys automatically to GitHub Pages.

[UPDATE] 1.7.1: Native Wayland & Controls Update — Wayland Compositors, Web Pointer Lock, and Polygon Gradients

Version 1.7.1 brings native Wayland support to Linux desktops alongside X11. The Linux binary dynamically detects Wayland compositors (such as GNOME and KDE Plasma) and runs with crisp, stutter-free native presentation, seamlessly falling back to X11 when run under an X11 session.

For the browser edition, mouse pointer locking and fullscreen toggling have been reworked: clicking either the Launch overlay or the canvas captures the pointer cleanly across modern browsers, pressing Esc releases the cursor without disrupting the session, and F11 or Alt+Enter toggles fullscreen presentation. On desktop builds, the F10 cursor capture toggle bug has been resolved.

Under the hood, this release fixes vertical scanline gradient rasterization in the 3D polygon engine, cleans up star coordinate hashing with clear descriptive naming, and clears all remaining pending items across the core engine.

[UPDATE] 1.8.0: The Acoustics & Surface Exploration Update — Maneuvering Acoustics, Cockpit Foley, and Surface Traversal

Version 1.8.0 expands the procedural acoustic soundscape of Noctis IV across spaceflight, cockpit operations, and planetary exploration without adding any external audio assets or audio files.

In flight, sublight attitude maneuvers now engage procedural cold-gas RCS thrusters with distinct valve pops and continuous directional hiss. Atmospheric descents produce deep resonant aerodynamic turbulence buffeting scaled dynamically to velocity and planetary air pressure, culminating in a heavy, dual-stage touchdown clunk when the landing gear contacts the ground.

Aboard the Stardrifter, the cockpit comes alive with vintage mechanical keyboard clacks while typing on the GOES console, stepped telemetry transmission chirps, harmonic acknowledge chimes, tactile dashboard rocker switches, linefeed scroll ticks, and observation deck elevator servo whines.

On planetary surfaces, the suit torch now directly illuminates terrain albedo and surfaces with realistic lighting rather than a flat overlay, footstep rhythms accurately track active walking locomotion, airless worlds feature tranquil spacesuit ventilation humming, and jetpack flight dynamics have been refined with smooth vertical velocity ceiling dampening and frictionless terrain clearance.

[UPDATE] 1.9.0: The Audio Controls & Exploration Ergonomics Update — Category Volume Sliders, Overlay Navigation, and Screenshot Ergonomics

Version 1.9.0 introduces comprehensive category-specific volume controls and critical exploration ergonomics across desktop and browser environments.

A new interactive High-DPI Audio Options overlay has been integrated into the graphics menu (F2 or Tab/A), providing five independent volume channels: Master, Spaceflight & Maneuvering (RCS, buffeting, touchdowns), Cockpit Foley (mechanical keyboard clacks, chirps, rocker switches, elevator servo), Visor & Suit (visor servos, breathing, ventilation), and Environment & Surface (wind, thunder, waves, footsteps). Players can seamlessly switch channels using mouse clicks, numeric shortcuts (1–5), or arrow keys, adjust levels with mouse drag or +/- keys, toggle global mute with M, and rely on automatic persistence in config.ini.

For astrophotographers and cartographers, cursor unlocking via F10 has been completely redesigned: releasing the mouse now instantly halts player locomotion and zeroes residual camera rotational momentum (dlt_alfa / dlt_beta), completely eliminating camera drift and ensuring external screenshot utilities capture perfectly framed views. All player inputs and keystrokes are safely gated while unfocused or unlocked, and cursor recentering warp jumps have been eliminated upon re-locking.
