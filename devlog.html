[TELEMETRY] System Development Log

INIT_DATE: September 27, 2026

SOURCE_REPOSITORY

Development on the modern Noctis port has reached an important turning point. Most of the recent work has been deep inside the machinery of the game: random-number generators, clocks, input adapters, palettes, and software polygon renderers.

The original DOS source remains our historical reference, while Noctis IV LR provides the modern C++ foundation.

[UPDATE] A Native Build We Can Trust

The Linux version can now be built consistently with two different C++ compilers. It has automated tests, startup diagnostics, and checks for memory errors and other unsafe behavior.

[UPDATE] Separating Time from Frame Rate

The simulation now advances in fixed steps, independently of how long the computer takes to present each frame. Drawing the screen and advancing the universe are treated as separate jobs.

[UPDATE] The Stardrifter Moves

The Stardrifter's interstellar and local guidance systems have been separated from the drawing loop and tested as part of the actual game code. For the first canonical journey, the ship leaves its inherited starting position and travels to BALASTRACKONASTREYA, the confirmed parent star of FELYSIA.

The interstellar leg takes exactly 396 simulation ticks. The local leg takes another 392. The trip also exercises the original power and lithium recharge behavior, ending with plenty of fuel in reserve.

[UPDATE] Down to the Ground and Back

The Stardrifter now has a repeatable round trip between orbit and the surface. A headless pilot requests a landing on FELYSIA, rides the capsule through gravity and touchdown, walks away across the generated terrain, returns to the capsule, and lets it seal and lift back aboard.

[UPDATE] The Stardrifter Answers Back

The cockpit has had a less glamorous but very useful refit. We mapped every inherited ship screen, every onboard menu, the three information panels in the flight display, and the four original preferences.

The modern keyboard bridge had another quiet limitation: it threw away everything except lowercase letters and digits. Printable text now arrives intact without being entered twice when Raylib reports it through two paths.

[UPDATE] GOESnet Comes Home

The Stardrifter no longer has to step out of the game and ask a folder of DOS programs what a command means. GOESnet now lives inside Noctis. Star and planet searches, travel targets, dependency listings, guide reading, note writing, catalog editing, star lists, exports, help, and screen clearing all pass through native components with one shared language.

[UPDATE] The Camera Is Rolling

Moviemaker is back. F3 now opens its control panel in space or on a planet, and a recording becomes a numbered “deck” of bitmap frames.

The native version treats old footage carefully. If a deck already exists, it refuses to record over it and asks for another number.

[UPDATE] Windows Calls In

The required Windows lane now builds cleanly and passes 34 automated tests. The deterministic stars and systems still agree, old saves still migrate, new saves survive restarts, GOESnet still edits its catalogs, Moviemaker still protects existing footage, and the scripted flights and landing state still arrive where expected.

[UPDATE] Hardening, Documentation, and 1.0 Readiness

Following the release of v0.0.1-preview and verification of the lithium persistence fix, Milestone 8 turned its focus toward comprehensive stabilization. We audited the software rasterizer to eliminate off-screen flare buffer overflows, corrected flare blending arithmetic, eliminated font glyph underflows, and added defensive bounds checks across the rendering pipeline.

Empirical profiling established strict performance budgets: the software rasterizer completes in under 2 milliseconds per frame, preserving over 96% headroom under the 55 ms DOS simulation tick. We expanded documentation with a comprehensive troubleshooting guide, contributor guidelines, and release runbooks. Release and rollback rehearsals passed cleanly for both Linux and Windows packages, preparing Noctis IV OM for 1.0 General Availability.

[UPDATE] 1.0.1 Patch: Smooth Landings, Starlight Floors, and Visor Controls

Following the 1.0 General Availability release, community exploration on low-gravity worlds like star Oakenshield’s first planet revealed an unexpected stall during descent. We investigated and established a solid vertical acceleration floor to guarantee smooth touchdown regardless of planetary mass, along with an instant Escape abort back to the cockpit.

We also illuminated planets around very dim stars that previously went pitch-black, protected the ship’s targeted body from power-loss resets while the pilot is outside, and restored the Page Up and Page Down controls for suit helmet visor operation and GOES guide scrolling.

[UPDATE] 1.1.0: Lighting the Torch — Nightside Illumination and Crisp HUD Telemetry

Exploring the unlit nightside of planets or tidally locked worlds no longer requires fumbling in total darkness. Version 1.1.0 introduces the planetary Suit Torch headlamp, toggled at any time with the L key. The headlamp casts an authentic spotlight beam directly in front of the explorer, blending ambient illumination, radial beam focus, quadratic distance attenuation, and smooth surface normal shading in 32-bit RGBA color space.

In addition, transient HUD status messages (such as TORCH ON/OFF and MOUSELOOK notifications) now render at crisp native display resolution directly over the retro framebuffer, eliminating text blur. Under the hood, surface session quitting via Escape now seamlessly synchronizes the ship’s orbital situation and guards celestial coordinates against cold-restart resets, ensuring your expeditions can be saved and resumed anywhere across the galaxy.
